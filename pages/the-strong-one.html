When Everyone Turns to You,
Who Do You Turn To?

A reflection on strength, vulnerability, and allowing yourself to be supported.

There is a particular kind of person we tend to admire.

They are dependable. They show up. They get things done. They are the person everyone calls when something goes wrong. They listen to other people's problems, offer advice, take care of what needs taking care of, and somehow keep moving even when life feels heavy.

They are “the strong one.”

Maybe you are that person.

And maybe, somewhere along the way, being strong stopped feeling like a choice and started feeling like an expectation. You became the person who doesn't fall apart—the person who figures things out, who doesn't need much from anyone.

From the outside, this looks like resilience. But there is a hidden cost to always holding everything together: You can become so accustomed to carrying your own weight that you forget you are allowed to put it down.

There is nothing wrong with being capable or independent. Being able to cope with difficult things is a strength, and being someone others can depend on can be deeply meaningful.

The problem begins when strength becomes something you feel you have to perform.

“I can handle this.”

Choice & agency

“I have to handle it.”

Fear, habit & isolation

When you operate from a place of having to handle everything, it sounds like:

  • 💭 “I should be able to handle this.”
  • 💭 “I'll deal with it later.”
  • 💭 “I don't want to burden anyone.”
  • 💭 “Other people have enough going on.”
  • 💭 “It's easier if I just do it myself.”
  • 💭 “I don't have time to fall apart.”

When strength becomes a requirement, there is very little room left for vulnerability.

When You’re Used to Being Strong

When you’re used to being strong, you become very good at suppressing your emotions.

Someone asks how you are doing, and “I'm fine” comes out before you've even had a chance to think about whether it's true. Eventually, you can become so practiced at pushing emotions aside that you lose touch with what is actually happening inside you.

🌊 The Emotional Water Glass

Imagine your emotions like water filling a container. You can keep putting the lid on, but eventually, the container becomes full. The feelings don't disappear—they spill over as irritability, anxiety, exhaustion, numbness, trouble sleeping, or sudden overwhelm.

Emotional health isn't about never feeling overwhelmed. It is about having enough space to notice what you're feeling, understand what you need, and respond with care.

And sometimes, that means admitting: “I'm not okay right now.”

Independence or Isolation?

There is a big difference between being independent and feeling like you cannot safely depend on anyone else.

Healthy Independence

“I know I can take care of myself.”

Hyper-Independence

“I have to take care of everything myself because the safest person to rely on is me.”

This belief doesn't appear out of nowhere. Perhaps you had to grow up quickly, or learned that depending on someone meant risking disappointment.

That self-sufficiency may have protected you once, but eventually, it prevents you from experiencing the very thing you need most: connection.

You can be surrounded by people who need you and still feel deeply alone.

Being needed can feel meaningful, but being needed isn't the same as being known. You deserve relationships where you don't only have a role as the helper, but where you can simply be a person.

Asking for Help Is a Muscle

Asking for help is a muscle. If you haven't used it in years, it feels uncomfortable—not because you don't need help, but because needing help feels like losing control.

⚡ Shift Your Perspective
Asking for help isn't a sign of weakness or failure. It means you recognize that being human comes with needs.

If you don't know where to start, try these simple sentences:

  • 🗣️ “I'm having a difficult day. Can I talk to you?”
  • 🗣️ “I don't need advice right now. I just need you to listen.”
  • 🗣️ “I don't have the capacity to take that on right now.”
  • 🗣️ “I'm struggling more than I've been letting on.”

You don't need a dramatic crisis to ask for help. You are allowed to ask simply because something is difficult.

Self-Regulation Isn’t Doing It Alone

When you need to self-regulate, it doesn't mean reverting to “I don't need anyone.” It means learning to stay present with yourself.

  • Anchor in the Body: Take a few slow breaths. Stretch. Take a warm shower. Put your feet on the floor.
  • Name the Emotion: Instead of “I'm having a terrible day,” try precision: “I am overwhelmed,” “I am lonely,” or “I am exhausted.”
  • Ask the Core Question: Switch from “How do I fix this?” to “What do I need right now?”

What We Were Taught

  • “Don't cry.”
  • “Be strong.”
  • “Handle it alone.”
  • “Don't be a burden.”

What We Can Learn

  • “My feelings count.”
  • “Asking is okay.”
  • “I can rest.”
  • “I can be supported.”

Old Belief: “Don't let people see you struggling.”
New Truth: “Being vulnerable requires courage. It allows me to be seen.”

Healing isn't about becoming less capable or abandoning your independence. It’s about giving yourself choices.

You can still be dependable. You can still solve problems. But you can also:

  • 🛑 Say “I don't have the capacity for this today.”
  • 🍲 Let someone else make dinner.
  • 🤲 Accept help without immediately trying to repay it.
  • 💧 Cry without apologizing.

None of this takes away your strength. It gives your strength somewhere to rest.

Sometimes the role of “the strong one” is so deeply ingrained that putting it down feels scary. Therapy provides a safe space to explore where these patterns came from and practice letting someone in.

Strength isn't about carrying the world on your shoulders—it’s trusting yourself enough to let yourself be supported.

📌 Remember
You are allowed to put the weight down sometimes. You can be capable and cared for. You can be independent and connected. You can be strong and human.
